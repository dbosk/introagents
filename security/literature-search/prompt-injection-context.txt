Research question: Can prompt injection against LLM-integrated applications and agents be reliably prevented? The claim under test: instructions and data share one channel in an LLM, so an application must assume that anything the model reads can steer it; prompt injection, including indirect prompt injection placed in retrieved data (web pages, e-mails, documents, tool outputs), works against real LLM-integrated applications and agents; published defences (input/output filtering, detection classifiers, prompt hardening such as delimiters or spotlighting, fine-tuned or instruction-hierarchy models) reduce attack success but have been bypassed by adaptive attacks; system-level designs that separate control flow from untrusted data (information-flow control, capabilities, privilege separation, dual-LLM or plan-then-execute patterns) can give guarantees for a class of attacks at some cost in utility; system prompts and other hidden context are easy to extract. Scope: 2022-2026, research papers and preprints.

A paper is relevant if it reports evidence (an experiment, a measurement, a formal proof, a systematic evaluation or a case study with real systems) on one of: (a) prompt injection or indirect prompt injection attacks against LLM-integrated applications or agents; (b) how a prompt-injection defence fares, in particular under adaptive or stronger attacks; (c) system-level or by-design defences and their guarantees and utility cost; (d) extraction or leaking of system prompts; or the separation of instructions and data in LLMs.

Category tags (exactly one per paper):
supports-claim = bears on the claim and supports it: shows prompt injection succeeding against applications or agents, defences that are insufficient or bypassed by adaptive attacks, that models cannot separate instructions from data, that system prompts are readily extracted, or that guarantees need constraining the agent's design at a cost in utility.
qualifies-claim = bears on the claim and contradicts or narrows it: reports a defence claimed to be robust, near-zero attack success, provably secure, or secure by design (also against adaptive attacks), a defence claimed to prevent prompt injection without utility loss, evidence that attacks rarely succeed or that the threat is overstated, or effective protection of system prompts.
adjacent-subtopic = LLM security but a different question (jailbreaks for harmful content, backdoors, data poisoning of training, privacy of training data, hallucination, using LLMs for security tasks) or no evidence of its own.
off-topic-false-hit = another field, keyword overlap only (SQL or code injection without LLMs, medical injections, agents in another sense, prompts in another sense).

Exclusion criteria; every paper that is not kept gets exactly one of these tags in addition to its category:
other-field = not about the security of LLMs or LLM-based systems (keyword overlap only).
jailbreak-only = about jailbreaking, harmful-content elicitation or other LLM risks, not about injected instructions in an application's data or prompt extraction.
llm-as-tool = uses an LLM for some task and mentions injection or security only in passing.
no-evidence = opinion, position, tutorial or overview without evidence or a stated method of its own.
no-abstract = the record has no abstract and none could be added.
other-question = about the security of LLMs or LLM-based systems, but a different question (backdoors, poisoning, denial of service, privacy, supply chain, forensics, scanning, over-defence), not injected instructions in an application's data or prompt extraction.
